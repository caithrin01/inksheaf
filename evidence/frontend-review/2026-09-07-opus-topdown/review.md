# Inksheaf — design critique

## 1. What the images actually show

**Strengths.** The material vocabulary is working. The engraved sheaf (Images 1, 3, 6) is the best asset in the set: a single incised mark, no gloss, no gradient — it earns the "burned into the wood" brief. The lamp falloff in Image 1 is believable. Type voice is restrained and consistent: transitional serif headline, italic single-line subhead, brick-red letterspaced small-caps label. Image 4 is the most credible screen you have, because it says real things: 22 essays, 179 estimated pages, a date range, an itemized price, "Nothing prints until you approve your proof. Readers order from Lulu," and an honest note that one archive post is excluded. That paragraph does more for trust than the whole desk scene.

**Visible faults, hero (Images 1–3).** The owner's objection is legible in the pixels. In Image 1 the headline, label, input and button are frontal, axis-aligned HTML sitting on a page that recedes to the right — two incompatible geometries in one rectangle.
