**Masthead presence — achieved, but hierarchy now tilts toward the vendor**

The reader header (Image 2) reads confidently: the wordmark at ~288px owns the upper-left, the rule beneath it separates brand from content, and the page title "Your book is taking shape." still dominates the eye because of its size and dark serif-free weight. That balance holds.

The homepage workspace block (Image 1) is where it slips. "inksheaf" is set heavier and roughly the same optical size as "caithrin · Annual 2025–26" directly beneath it, so on a page whose subject is someone else's book, the platform name reads as the title and the publication as a subtitle. That's the one place the request risks drowning the creator's work — not on the cover art (correctly untouched), but in the naming hierarchy of the workspace.

**Watermark — deliberate in intent, slightly heavy in execution**

Placement logic is sound and legible as a choice, not an accident: consistent baseline on both sheets, bottom-anchored, restricted to opening/closing matter, folio clearance corrected. The lift on the closing note (Image 5) works, though the clearance now looks tight — the descender-free baseline sits close enough to the "3" that the two read as one cluster rather than two independent elements.

The bigger issue is mass. At 4.1in on a 6in page the mark occupies about two-thirds of the trim width, and even at #E9EAE7 that much area accumulates into a perceptible pale band across the foot. On the half-title (Image 4), where the only other ink is a single short line of type, the watermark is the larger visual object on the page; the creator's title floats while the imprint anchors. It doesn't overwhelm, but it's closer to "second title" than "quiet ghost." I can't judge how it will actually lie on stock — screen renderings of near-white tints are unreliable, and no print check is implied here.

**Gradient — reads as very subtle**

Across all three screens I cannot detect a directional wash without looking for it; the work surface reads as one neutral tone. That matches the brief. A still cannot show banding on wide gradients, dark-mode behaviour, or that high-contrast and print removal actually fire — those remain unverified, as does the claim that PDF pixels are untouched. As shown, subtlety is respected. The visible outline on the Next button is genuine focus and should stay.

**Three improvements**

1. **Subordinate the wordmark to the publication in the workspace block.** Keep the larger mark, but reduce its weight or step it down relative to "caithrin · Annual 2025–26", or promote the publication line to the dominant size. The owner asked for prominence in the corner masthead, not for the imprint to outrank the author's title in body content.

2. **Reduce the watermark's mass rather than move it.** Try ~3.4–3.6in wide, or lighten roughly one step (toward #EDEEEB), and add a little more clearance above the folio on the closing page so the mark and the page number don't read as a single unit. Aim for the mark being the second thing noticed on the half-title, not the first.

3. **Check the phone header for collision.** In Image 3 "YOUR PRIVATE EDITION" wraps to two lines and sits very close to the enlarged mark's right edge; at the 210px end of the range that gap will get tighter. Cap the phone mark nearer 166–180px, or move the edition label below the rule.

**Scope note**

Everything reviewed is a local fixture and a synthetic nine-page specimen; the word counts, page counts and middle-page pixel parity claims are outside this critique and untested by me. The owner's actual annual PDF remains held. This is a design review only — not approval, and not release acceptance.
