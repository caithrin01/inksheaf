A Reader’s Questions

answered, as you’d find them at the back of the book

“So what does this cost me?”

Nothing, now or at launch. Your readers pay the printer, about the price of a good paperback (a 294-page copy came to $16.58). Whether a little sits on top for you is your call, and it’s yours to keep.

“Do I still own my words?”

Every one. It’s your book: your name on the cover, your name on the spine. We sign our work exactly once, small, on the back — printed by Inksheaf.

“How much of my weekend is this?”

Paste your address, approve the proof. We handle the rest: the contents, the running heads, the notes, the quiet trick of making two hundred posts open flat.

“What actually goes in?”

Every public essay, in the order you wrote them. Your paid posts stay private unless you hand us the export yourself. Nothing sneaks in.

“What does it feel like in the hand?”

Six by nine, perfect bound, a matte cover in the old literary style. Uncoated paper that takes the light. It feels like a book because it is one.

“How do my readers get one?”

A single link at the foot of a post, or on your About page. They click, the printer prints and ships, the book turns up at their door. You’re never in the middle.

“My archive is enormous.”

Then it’s a set — handsome volumes instead of one doorstop. A quarterly, an annual, whatever fits the shelf.
